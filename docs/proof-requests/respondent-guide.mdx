# Respondent Guide

As the respondent, you receive a 6-digit code from the requester, submit your evidence privately, and confirm submission. The requester receives only the risk assessment result — they cannot read your evidence.

## Responding to a Proof Request

<Steps>
  <Step title="Receive the code">
    The requester shares a 6-digit code with you through any channel — message, email, phone call, or in person. You need this code to submit your evidence.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Open InvisiProof">
    Open the InvisiProof app. You do not need to have an account to respond to a Proof Request — you can respond as a guest.

    <Note>
    If you respond as a guest, your evidence is still stored privately and the requester still cannot read it. However, you will not be able to view your submission history without an account.
    </Note>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Navigate to Requests">
    Tap the **Requests** tab at the bottom of the screen.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Enter the code">
    Tap **Enter Code** and type or paste the 6-digit code you received.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Read the challenge">
    Read the challenge set by the requester carefully. It explains:
    - What they are asking you to submit
    - Why they are asking
    - What the evidence will be used for

    If the challenge is unclear or you are uncomfortable with it, you do not have to submit. You can close the screen without submitting.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Submit your evidence">
    Choose the type of evidence to submit:

    - **Image** — select from your photo library or take a photo
    - **Text** — paste the message, email, or conversation
    - **URL** — enter the link

    Review the consent notice. It explains that your evidence will be stored privately, analyzed by AI, and that the requester will receive only the risk assessment result — not your evidence.

    Tap **Submit Evidence** to confirm.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Confirmation">
    You will see a confirmation screen indicating that your evidence was submitted successfully. The requester will be notified when the analysis is complete.

    You will not see the risk assessment result — only the requester sees that.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Your privacy as a respondent

<Note>
Your evidence is stored privately in Supabase Storage with row-level security policies that prevent the requester from reading it through the API. This is enforced at the database level — it is not just a UI restriction.
</Note>

| What the requester sees | What the requester CANNOT see |
|------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Risk level and score | Your image, text, or URL |
| Warning indicators | Your name or account information |
| Recommended actions | Any other requests you have responded to |
| Trust level | Your submission history |

## What happens to your evidence

- Your evidence is stored in a private Supabase Storage bucket scoped to the proof request
- It is analyzed by AI to produce the risk assessment
- After the request is completed, the evidence remains stored until you or the requester deletes the request, or until the retention period expires
- You can request deletion of your evidence by contacting support@invisiproof.com

## Before you submit

Consider these questions before submitting evidence:

1. **Do you recognize the requester?** You should only respond to Proof Request codes from people you know and trust.
2. **Is the challenge reasonable?** The challenge should explain a legitimate reason for the request.
3. **Are you comfortable with AI analysis?** Your evidence will be analyzed by an AI model. Review the [AI Disclaimer](/legal/ai-disclaimer) if you have concerns.
4. **Does the evidence contain sensitive information?** If the evidence contains highly sensitive personal information (medical records, financial account details, etc.), consider whether you are comfortable submitting it.

<Warning>
Only respond to Proof Request codes from people you know. Do not respond to codes shared by strangers, as this could be used to trick you into submitting sensitive content.
</Warning>
