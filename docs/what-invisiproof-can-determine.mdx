# What InvisiProof Can and Cannot Determine

Understanding the boundaries of InvisiProof's analysis is essential for interpreting results correctly. This page explains what signals the system detects, what it cannot determine, and when to seek professional advice.

<Warning>
InvisiProof results are not legal evidence, professional opinions, or guarantees of safety or harm. A Low Risk result does not mean content is safe. A High Risk result does not mean content is definitely harmful. Always use results as one input among many.
</Warning>

## What InvisiProof can assess

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Visual signals in images">
    When you submit an image or screenshot, InvisiProof looks for:

    - **Compression artifacts** — unusual JPEG/PNG compression patterns that may indicate the image was re-saved after editing
    - **Layout and spacing anomalies** — inconsistent fonts, misaligned elements, or spacing that differs from authentic templates
    - **Text within images** — names, amounts, dates, account numbers, and instructions embedded in the image
    - **Structural patterns** — layouts common in fraudulent documents, fake invoices, or manipulated screenshots
    - **Color and contrast inconsistencies** — areas of the image that appear to have been composited or altered
    - **Metadata signals** — where available, patterns in image metadata that suggest editing history
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Linguistic patterns in text">
    When you submit a text message, email, or conversation, InvisiProof looks for:

    - **Urgency language** — phrases designed to pressure immediate action ("act now", "your account will be closed", "limited time")
    - **Impersonation patterns** — language mimicking banks, government agencies, tech companies, or known individuals
    - **Financial pressure signals** — requests for payment, gift cards, wire transfers, or cryptocurrency
    - **Suspicious instructions** — requests to click links, download files, provide credentials, or keep communication secret
    - **Structural anomalies** — unusual formatting, inconsistent sender information, or mismatched context
    - **Emotional manipulation** — fear, urgency, flattery, or romantic pressure used to override judgment
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="URL structure signals">
    When you submit a URL, InvisiProof analyzes it structurally without visiting it from your device:

    - **Domain structure** — subdomains designed to mimic legitimate domains (e.g. `paypal.com.attacker.net`)
    - **URL length and complexity** — unusually long URLs with encoded parameters common in phishing
    - **Known pattern matching** — structural patterns associated with phishing kits, URL shorteners, or redirect chains
    - **TLD signals** — top-level domains statistically associated with higher abuse rates
    - **Redirect indicators** — URL structures that suggest multi-hop redirects
    - **Typosquatting patterns** — domains that closely resemble well-known brands with minor character substitutions
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## What InvisiProof cannot determine

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Legal authenticity" icon="gavel">
    InvisiProof cannot confirm whether a document, signature, or transaction is legally valid or legally fraudulent.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Identity" icon="user">
    InvisiProof cannot verify who sent a message, who created an image, or whether a person is who they claim to be.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Intent" icon="brain">
    InvisiProof cannot determine whether a person intended to deceive. High-risk signals may appear in legitimate content.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Completeness" icon="magnifying-glass">
    InvisiProof cannot detect all forms of deception. Sophisticated manipulation may produce low-risk scores.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

InvisiProof also **cannot**:
- Replace a forensic investigation by a qualified professional
- Provide legal advice or serve as expert testimony
- Guarantee that a URL is safe to visit
- Confirm that a transaction will succeed or fail
- Identify the geographic origin of content

## Confidence levels

Every assessment includes a risk score from 0 to 100. The score reflects the density and severity of observable signals — it is not a probability of fraud.

| Score Range | Risk Level | What it means |
|-------------|------------|---------------|
| 0–24 | Low | Few or no risk signals detected in observable content |
| 25–49 | Medium | Some risk signals present; warrants caution |
| 50–74 | High | Multiple significant risk signals; take protective action |
| 75–100 | Critical | Strong concentration of high-severity risk signals |

<Note>
A score of 0 does not mean content is safe. It means the observable signals analyzed did not trigger risk indicators. Sophisticated deception may evade detection.
</Note>

## When to seek professional advice

Seek professional advice when:

- The stakes are high (financial transactions, legal matters, personal safety)
- The risk level is High or Critical
- You are considering legal action based on the content
- The content involves a child or vulnerable person
- You need results that will hold up in court or a formal investigation

For legal matters, consult a qualified attorney. For financial fraud, contact your bank and relevant authorities. For personal safety concerns, contact local law enforcement.
