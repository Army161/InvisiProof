# AI Disclaimer

InvisiProof uses artificial intelligence to analyze submitted content and produce risk assessments. This disclaimer explains the limitations of AI-powered analysis and how to interpret results responsibly.

<Warning>
InvisiProof is not a legal service. Assessment results are not legal evidence, expert opinions, or guarantees of safety or harm. Do not make high-stakes decisions based solely on InvisiProof results.
</Warning>

---

## What AI analysis can and cannot do

### What it can do

- Identify observable patterns in images, text, and URLs that are statistically associated with fraud, phishing, or manipulation
- Produce a normalized risk score based on the density and severity of detected signals
- Generate plain-language descriptions of what was observed
- Suggest appropriate next steps based on the risk level

### What it cannot do

- **Determine intent** — AI cannot determine whether a person intended to deceive
- **Verify identity** — AI cannot confirm who created or sent content
- **Guarantee accuracy** — AI models make mistakes. Sophisticated deception may evade detection. Legitimate content may trigger false positives
- **Provide legal conclusions** — AI analysis is not a substitute for forensic investigation or legal expertise
- **Detect all forms of manipulation** — new techniques may not be in the model's training data
- **Account for context you have** — the AI does not know your relationship with the sender, the history of your communication, or other context that might change the interpretation

---

## AI limitations

### False negatives (missed detections)

A Low Risk result does not mean content is safe. The AI may fail to detect:
- Novel phishing techniques not represented in training data
- Highly sophisticated document manipulation
- Social engineering that relies on context the AI doesn't have
- Manipulation in languages or formats the model handles less well

### False positives (incorrect flags)

A High Risk or Critical result does not mean content is definitely harmful. The AI may incorrectly flag:
- Legitimate urgent communications from banks or employers
- Documents with unusual but legitimate formatting
- URLs with unusual but legitimate structures
- Text that uses urgency language for legitimate reasons

### Model variability

AI models can produce different results for the same content at different times. Results may vary based on:
- The specific model version in use
- Server load and processing conditions
- Minor variations in how content is processed

## How to interpret results responsibly

1. **Read the warning indicators** — they explain what specific signals were detected, not just the overall score
2. **Read the limitations section** — every assessment includes explicit statements about what it cannot determine
3. **Apply your own judgment** — you have context the AI doesn't. Use the result as one input, not the only input
4. **Seek verification for high-stakes decisions** — if the decision involves significant money, legal consequences, or personal safety, get independent verification
5. **Don't over-rely on Low Risk results** — a Low Risk result means the observable signals don't indicate elevated risk, not that the content is safe
