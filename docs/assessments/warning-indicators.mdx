# Warning Indicators

Warning indicators are specific observable signals found in the submitted content. They explain *why* a particular risk level was assigned.

## What warning indicators are

Each assessment may include one or more warning indicators. Each indicator describes a specific pattern or signal that was observed. Examples include:

- **Urgency language detected** — The message uses time pressure to push you toward a quick decision
- **Credential request** — The content asks for a password, PIN, or login information
- **Impersonation pattern** — The content mimics a known brand, institution, or person
- **Suspicious domain structure** — The URL uses patterns associated with phishing (e.g., brand name in subdomain)
- **Visual inconsistency** — The image shows signs of editing or layout anomalies
- **Unusual payment request** — The content requests payment via gift cards, wire transfer, or cryptocurrency
- **Mismatch detected** — The claimed identity or context does not match observable signals

## How to read warning indicators

Warning indicators are not proof of fraud or harm. They are signals that appear more frequently in higher-risk content. A single indicator does not mean the content is dangerous. Multiple indicators increase the overall risk signal.

Read each indicator alongside the summary and recommended actions to understand the full picture.

## What to do with warning indicators

- Use them to ask better questions before acting
- Verify specific claims through independent channels (call the company directly, check official websites)
- Do not share warning indicators as proof of wrongdoing — they are risk signals, not evidence

## When no indicators are shown

If no warning indicators appear, the assessment did not find specific signals to highlight. This does not mean the content is safe — it means the observable signals did not trigger specific pattern matches.
